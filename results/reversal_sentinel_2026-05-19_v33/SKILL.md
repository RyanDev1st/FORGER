# gnosis-reversal-sentinel

## Invocation
Use when current consensus may be fragile because past consensus flipped, deployments contradicted theory, incentives changed, or hidden boundary conditions emerged.

## Pipeline

### Step 1 — Parse Reversal Frame
Extract:
- central claim
- current consensus
- proposed action
- past dominant view
- possible flip conditions
- deployment or adoption boundary
- stakes if consensus reverses

### Step 2 — Spawn Lanes
Spawn Scholar, Community, and Edge in parallel. Each lane emits reversal cards plus findings.

### Step 3 — Validate
Run V1–V8:
- V1 file exists and non-empty.
- V2 at least 5 reversal cards or pivot log.
- V3 no top-level refusal.
- V4 every card has source and verbatim quote.
- V5 every card names reversal direction.
- V6 every card names reversal mechanism.
- V7 every card recommends trust, hedge, test, or reject.
- V8 closing block present.

### Step 4 — Audit
Check URLs, quote matches, reversal chronology, and Edge redundancy. Preserve failures as downgrade flags.

### Step 5 — Build Reversal Map
Classify cards into:
- confirmed reversal
- partial reversal
- failed reversal claim
- deployment-only reversal
- incentive-driven reversal
- measurement-driven reversal
- scope-bound reversal
- sentinel warning

### Step 6 — Synthesize
Return:
- current claim durability
- reversal mechanisms to monitor
- consensus zones safe to trust
- claims needing hedged recommendation
- testable sentinel conditions

### Step 7 — Re-fan
Re-fan only when reversal mechanism could change recommendation. One round max unless user escalates.

## Output
Return:
1. Reversal map.
2. Trust/hedge/test/reject decisions.
3. Current synthesis.
4. Reversal hot spots.
5. Sentinel conditions.
6. Evidence appendix.

## Constraints
- No reversal claim without chronology.
- No treating contrarian disagreement as reversal without adoption or evidence shift.
- Maximum four concurrent threads.
- Preserve raw evidence appendices.
